\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Consider a device sitting in one of two hidden states. Pressing button~$L$ repairs it in the first state but irreversibly breaks it in the second; button~$R$ does the exact opposite. The engineer also has a third option—running a diagnostic probe: it reveals the true state without error, yet in the second state the diagnostic procedure itself irreversibly breaks the device. A striking paradox emerges: a repair exists for each individual state, yet \emph{no single strategy} guarantees success without prior knowledge of the state—and no amount of additional time, memory, or computation can overcome this barrier. There is no noise and no quantum mechanics here: the entire system is an ordinary finite deterministic automaton. The core shift is that \textbf{the ability to acquire knowledge and the ability to act upon it decouple}: the very act of acquiring information can destroy the ability to reach the goal (\textbf{epistemic deformation of reachability}, $L_H^{\mathrm{EDR}}$). This raises two foundational questions in decision theory and the ethics of action: \emph{is it worth trying to learn the truth when the act of learning irreversibly alters the world and closes the path to the right action?} And \emph{how does rational responsibility change when choice begins before action—and even before the facts are obtained?}

Formally, this paper studies not surface similarity across domains, but the \emph{consistent logical irreducibility of a joint entailment} $\Theta_M\models h$ with $\forall A\subsetneq M:\ \Theta_A\not\models h$: impossibility arises only when three constraints compose—an irreversible trap ($G_1$), observational indistinguishability through a passive port ($G_2$), and state-deforming observation ($G_4$)—whereas removing any single one restores a constructive winning policy (ablation countermodels $C_1,C_2,C_4$). In a self-contained, zero-import Lean~4.36.0 module (\texttt{epistemic\_\allowbreak deformation\_\allowbreak reachability\_\allowbreak v3\_\allowbreak 2.lean}, 27~declarations, \texttt{exit 0}, 0~\texttt{sorryAx}, 0~\texttt{native\_\allowbreak decide}), we prove: (1)~reduction of consistent irreducibility to maximal proper subsets (\texttt{CrossCore}); (2)~three separate native domain signatures (\texttt{SinkGraph}, \texttt{LinearProjectionF2} with $\mathbb{F}_2^2$-linearity, and \texttt{AuditBackAction}) linked by glue-parameterized \texttt{Satisfaction\allowbreak Bridge}s and a load-bearing \texttt{InterfaceGlue}; (3)~unbounded-horizon impossibility across all $k\in\mathbb{N}$ (\texttt{joint\_\allowbreak entailment\_\allowbreak all\_\allowbreak horizons}, axioms \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Quot.sound}); (4)~Boolean independence of all 8~vectors of $\Phi_3'$ and the \textbf{anti-inflation theorem} ($U_P(s_1)=s_0$); (5)~a general belief-space characterization \texttt{SafeBeliefReach} with a closed formal bridge \texttt{PlanTree}~$\leftrightarrow$~\texttt{Policy} preserving the passive port $\pi$; and (6)~strictly negative probabilistic value of information ($\mathrm{VoI}_{\mathrm{EDR}}=-1/3<0$) contrasted against discrete Blackwell monotonicity. The verified result carries the strict license \texttt{FORMAL\_\allowbreak CROSS\_\allowbreak IRREDUCIBILITY} and is kept explicitly separate from \texttt{INDEPENDENT\_\allowbreak CROSS\_\allowbreak DISCOVERY}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Statement: Two Limits of ``Knowing More \texorpdfstring{$\not\Rightarrow$}{Does Not Imply} Doing More''}

In classical statistical decision theory (Blackwell~\cite{blackwell1953}) and Savage's axiomatization~\cite{savage1954}, a free experiment refines the partition of hidden states without shrinking the set of achievable outcomes ($\mathrm{VoI}\ge 0$). Violations of this monotonicity in operational systems have at least two orthogonal sources:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Causal-temporal horizon closure (\texttt{v1.5} / \texttt{v2.6.1}~\cite{strand_v15,strand_v261}).} Observation does not deform the state directly, but takes time $\Delta t>0$ during which the environment evolves, pushing the actuator outside the backward light cone of the target by deadline $t^*$. Removing the deadline removes the barrier.
  \item \textbf{Epistemic deformation of reachability (\texttt{v3.2}).} There is no deadline at all ($k\in\mathbb{N}$ is arbitrarily large), yet the observation probe $P$ induces state back-action ($U_P\ne\mathrm{id}$). When an incomplete passive projection ($G_2$) forces the agent to disambiguate hidden states via probing, and probing moves one state to a designated destination ($G_4$) that is dynamically an absorbing trap ($G_1$), guaranteed goal achievement becomes impossible at \emph{every} horizon.
\end{enumerate}

Consider a device in one of two hidden states ($s_0$ or $s_1$) with three available operations:

Acting blindly on the first step ($L$ or $R$) breaks one of the two worlds; running diagnostics $P$ identifies the world without error, yet in state $s_1$ nothing remains repairable. Removing \emph{any single} constraint—allowing recovery from $q$ (dropping $G_1$), separating $s_0$ and $s_1$ passively (dropping $G_2$), or making $P$ non-destructive (dropping $G_4$)—immediately restores a winning strategy.

To avoid conflating cross-domain synthesis with verbal analogy~\cite{gentner1983}, we formulate the criterion via \emph{consistent logical irreducibility} over separate native signatures connected by satisfaction-preserving bridges in the spirit of institutions~\cite{goguen1992}.

\section{Common Signature \texorpdfstring{$L_H^{\mathrm{EDR}}$}{L\_H\^{}EDR} and Irreducibility Reduction (\texorpdfstring{\texttt{CrossCore}}{CrossCore})}

\begin{definition}[Monotone Entailment and Consistent Logical Irreducibility]
Let $D$ be a type of domain identifiers with decidable equality, and let $E=(\mathrm{entails},\mathrm{mono})$ be a monotone entailment relation over finite lists of domains ($A\subseteq B\implies E.\mathrm{entails}(A)\to E.\mathrm{entails}(B)$). A list $A$ is a proper subset of $M$ ($\mathrm{IsProperSubset}(A,M)$) if $\forall x\in A,\ x\in M$ and $\exists d\in M,\ d\notin A$. We say $M$ is \emph{logically irreducible} ($\mathrm{IsIrreducible}(E,M)$) if $E.\mathrm{entails}(M)$ and $\forall A,\ \mathrm{IsProperSubset}(A,M)\to\neg E.\mathrm{entails}(A)$, and \emph{consistently irreducible} ($\mathrm{IsConsistentIrreducible}(E,\mathrm{Consistent},M)$) if in addition $\mathrm{Consistent}(M)$ holds.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Reduction of Irreducibility to Maximal Proper Subsets, \texttt{crossCore\_\allowbreak irreducible\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak maximal}]
\label{thm:crosscore}
For any monotone entailment $E$ and domain list $M$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{IsIrreducible}(E,M) \iff \Bigl(E.\mathrm{entails}(M) \;\land\; \forall d\in M:\ \neg E.\mathrm{entails}(M\setminus\{d\})\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

For $n$ domains, Theorem~\ref{thm:crosscore} (proved in Lean~4 using only \texttt{propext}) reduces checking all $2^n-1$ proper subsets to exhibiting $n$ countermodels for maximal proper subsets $M\setminus\{d\}$. Pairing this with $\mathrm{Consistent}(M)$ in \texttt{IsConsistentIrreducible} rules out vacuous \emph{ex falso} minimal inconsistent sets (structurally dual to minimal unsatisfiable subsets and Reiter's minimal diagnoses~\cite{reiter1987}).

We fix the common finite signature
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma = \langle S,\ O,\ \Act,\ \ObsAct,\ \Win,\ \pi,\ \delta,\ \omega_{\mathrm{obs}} \rangle,
\end{equation}
where $S=\{s_0,s_1,w,q\}$, $O=\mathrm{Bool}$, $\Act=\{L,R\}$, $\ObsAct=\{P\}$, $\Win:S\to\mathrm{Bool}$, passive projection $\pi:S\to\mathrm{Bool}$, control transition $\delta:S\times\Act\to S$, and active probe $\omega_{\mathrm{obs}}(s,P)=(U_P(s),Y_P(s))$. A deterministic history-dependent policy has type $\mathrm{Policy}:=(\mathrm{Bool}\times\mathrm{List}(\mathrm{Decision}\times\mathrm{Bool}))\to\mathrm{Decision}$, where $\mathrm{Decision}\in\{\mathrm{act}(L),\mathrm{act}(R),\mathrm{probe},\mathrm{stop}\}$. Policies never observe the hidden state $s\in S$: they receive $\pi(s)$ initially and the sequence of past decisions and observations thereafter.

For a fixed initial belief $B_0=\{s_0,s_1\}$, $\GuaranteedWin(\mathrm{glue},W,\mathrm{pol},k)$ asserts that executing $\mathrm{pol}$ from both aligned initial states reaches $\Win=\mathsf{true}$ within $\le k$ steps. Horizon-$k$ impossibility is $h_k(W):\iff\forall\mathrm{pol}:\ \neg\GuaranteedWin(\mathrm{glue},W,\mathrm{pol},k)$, and unbounded-horizon impossibility is $\mathrm{ClaimHAll}(\mathrm{glue},W):\iff\forall k\in\mathbb{N},\ h_k(W)$.

\section{Three Separate Domain Signatures, \texorpdfstring{\texttt{InterfaceGlue}}{InterfaceGlue}, and \texorpdfstring{\texttt{Satisfaction\allowbreak Bridge}}{SatisfactionBridge}}

To separate domain properties from a monolithic transition table, \texttt{epistemic\_\allowbreak deformation\_\allowbreak reachability\_\allowbreak v3\_\allowbreak 2.lean} defines three independent native structures:

Theorem \texttt{sinkGraph\_\allowbreak trap\_\allowbreak never\_\allowbreak reaches\_\allowbreak win} (0~axioms) proves by induction that no finite action sequence leaves $\mathrm{trap}$.
  \item \textbf{$G_2$ (\texttt{LinearProjectionF2}):} a map $\mathrm{proj}:\mathbb{F}_2^2\to\mathbb{F}_2$ over the canonical encoding $s_0=(0,0),\ s_1=(0,1),\ q=(1,0),\ w=(1,1)$ (bijection \texttt{decode\_\allowbreak encode\_\allowbreak bit2}, \texttt{encode\_\allowbreak decode\_\allowbreak bit2}, 0~axioms). \section{Boolean Independence of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_3'$}{Phi3'} and the Anti-Inflation Theorem}

\section{General \texorpdfstring{\texttt{SafeBeliefReach}}{SafeBeliefReach} Characterization and Negative Probabilistic Value of Information}

\subsection{Belief-Space Characterization and the Formal \texorpdfstring{\texttt{PlanTree}~$\leftrightarrow$~\texttt{Policy}}{PlanTree <-> Policy} Bridge}

For any finite system $\mathrm{GenEDRWorld}(S,O,\Act,\ObsAct)$ equipped with a \emph{passive observation port} $\pi:S\to O$, transitions $\delta$, probe back-action $U_p$, and probe outcome $Y_p$, we filter active (not-yet-won) states $B_{\mathrm{act}}=\{s\in B\mid\Win(s)=\mathsf{false}\}$ and define a recursive belief-space AND--OR reachability game (a finite-horizon deterministic viability kernel~\cite{aubin1991,bonet2000}):
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,0,B) &:\iff \forall s\in B,\ \Win(s)=\mathsf{true}, \\
  \mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,k+1,B) &:\iff (\forall s\in B,\ \Win(s)=\mathsf{true}) \nonumber\\
  &\quad\lor\; \bigl(\exists a\in\Act:\ \forall y\in O,\ \mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,k,B_{a,y}^{\mathrm{act}})\bigr) \nonumber\\
  &\quad\lor\; \bigl(\exists p\in\ObsAct:\ \forall y\in O,\ \mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,k,B_{p,y}^{\mathrm{prb}})\bigr),
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{SafeBeliefReachInit}(W,k,B):\iff\forall o\in O,\ \mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,k,B_{\pi=o})$.

\begin{theorem}[Controllability Characterization and \texttt{PlanTree} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{Policy} Bridge]
\label{thm:sbr}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{General equivalence (\texttt{safeBeliefReach\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak validPlan}, \texttt{[Classical.choice]}):} for any system $W$ with passive port $\pi$, horizon $k\in\mathbb{N}$, and belief $B$, $\mathrm{SafeBeliefReach}(W,k,B)$ holds iff there exists a valid finite decision tree $t:\mathrm{PlanTree}(\Act,\ObsAct,O)$.
  \item \textbf{Formal bridge to functional policies (\texttt{safeBeliefReachInit\_\allowbreak iff\_\allowbreak exists\_\allowbreak policy}, \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}):} for any $W:\mathrm{EDRWorld}$ and $k\in\mathbb{N}$, $\mathrm{SafeBeliefReachInit}(\allowbreak\mathrm{toGenEDR}\,W,\allowbreak k,\allowbreak [s_0,s_1])$ holds iff there exists a deterministic history-dependent policy $\mathrm{pol}:\mathrm{Hist}\to\mathrm{Decision}$ satisfying $\GuaranteedWin(\allowbreak\mathrm{edrInterfaceGlue},\allowbreak W,\allowbreak \mathrm{pol},\allowbreak k)$.
  \item \textbf{Full 5-model separation (\texttt{safeBeliefReachInit\_\allowbreak separates\_\allowbreak all\_\allowbreak models}):} $\mathrm{SafeBeliefReachInit}$ fails for \texttt{worldAll} at every $k\in\mathbb{N}$, and holds for \texttt{worldC1} ($k=2$), \texttt{worldC2} ($k=1$, via preserved $\pi$), \texttt{worldC4} ($k=2$), and \texttt{worldAntiInflation} ($k=2$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Discrete Blackwell Monotonicity vs.\ Strictly Negative Value of Information}

Under Blackwell's theorem~\cite{blackwell1953} and Savage's state-act independence~\cite{savage1954}, passive observation ($U_P=\mathrm{id}$) never reduces expected success. Under epistemic deformation, this inequality reverses. Consider the 3-world system \texttt{voiWorld3} with uniform prior $\mu=(1,1,1)$ on $\{s_0,s_1,s_2\}$: action $L$ repairs $s_0,s_1$ (and breaks $s_2$), action $R$ repairs only $s_2$, and probe $P$ identifies all three worlds ($o_0,o_1,o_2$) with 100\% accuracy while sending $s_1$ and $s_2$ into absorbing trap $q$.

\begin{theorem}[Blackwell Monotonicity and Strictly Negative $\mathrm{VoI}_{\mathrm{EDR}}$]
\label{thm:voi}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Discrete Blackwell monotonicity (\texttt{passive\_\allowbreak probe\_\allowbreak voi\_\allowbreak nonneg}, \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}):} for any state list $B$, integer prior weights $\mu:S\to\mathbb{N}$, and binary passive probe $p:S\to\mathrm{Bool}$ ($U_p=\mathrm{id}$), $\mathrm{bestBlindMass}(B)\le\mathrm{bestBlindMass}(B_{p=1})+\mathrm{bestBlindMass}(B_{p=0})$ ($\mathrm{VoI}\ge 0$).
  \item \textbf{Strictly negative value of information (\texttt{deforming\_\allowbreak probe\_\allowbreak strict\_\allowbreak negative\_\allowbreak voi}, \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}):} on \texttt{voiWorld3}, probe $P$ is injective on $\{s_0,s_1,s_2\}$, yet blind $L$ wins mass $2/3$, whereas post-probe branch $o_0$ wins ($1/3$) and branches $o_1,o_2$ fail at \emph{every} horizon $k\in\mathbb{N}$, giving $\mathrm{VoI}_{\mathrm{EDR}}=1/3-2/3=-1/3<0$.
  \item \textbf{Randomized blind dominance (\texttt{randomized\_\allowbreak blind\_\allowbreak beats\_\allowbreak post\_\allowbreak probe\_\allowbreak ceiling}, \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}):} for any exact rational blind mixture $(p_L,p_R)$ with $p_L>0$, expected blind winning mass $2p_L+p_R$ strictly exceeds the universal post-probe trap ceiling $p_L+p_R$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The Second-Order Trolley Problem and Contrast with the Prisoner's Dilemma}

The classical trolley problem examines a \emph{conflict of values under complete knowledge}: all facts are known in advance, and observation is free and neutral. Epistemic deformation moves the dilemma one step earlier—into a ``second-order trolley problem.'' Suppose five people are on one track and none on the other, but the agent does not know which track is which; a scanner reveals the truth with certainty, yet in one state running the scanner itself locks the switch onto the fatal track. Responsibility splits into three layers: whether to observe at all, which action to take afterward, and accountability for the part of the world altered by the observation. In Pearl's causal language~\cite{pearl2009}, observation ceases to be passive reading and becomes an intervention $\mathrm{do}(P)$.

In the one-shot \emph{Prisoner's Dilemma}, the obstacle is a \emph{conflict of incentives between two agents}: even knowing the partner's move, each player has a strictly dominant strategy to defect. In epistemic deformation there is a single agent and a single undisputed goal, and the barrier arises not from monetary or temporal test cost, but from structural deformation of the reachable state space (down to $\mathrm{VoI}_{\mathrm{EDR}}=-1/3<0$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:voi}).
